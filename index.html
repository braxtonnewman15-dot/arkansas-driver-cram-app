Start here: how to cram in two days

Important: This is a comprehensive condensed study aid organized around the headings in the July 2026, Edition 10 state guide. It is not a verbatim reproduction of every page, picture, or legal citation. Use the official manual to confirm details; the official 25-question practice test is the best final check.
SATURDAY

Build the foundation — about 4 hours total

  1. Licenses, testing documents, and Chapter 1 Arkansas laws (45 min)
  2. Chapter 2: signals, signs, pavement markings, work zones, railroads (75 min)
  3. Chapter 3: lanes, passing, turning, right-of-way, roundabouts, parking (60 min)
  4. Chapter 4: operating safely, distraction, awareness, blind spots (45 min)
  5. Do the 25-question practice set and record every miss (15–30 min)
SUNDAY

Connect the rules and test — about 4 hours total

  1. Chapter 5 visibility, lights, speeds, distance, safety cushion (60 min)
  2. Chapters 6–8: trucks, bikes/motorcycles, driver fitness, alcohol/drugs (60 min)
  3. Chapter 9 emergency handling and crash duties; Chapter 10 skills checklist (45 min)
  4. Flashcards and targeted review of missed lessons (30 min)
  5. Take a full practice round; finish with signs and numbers (45 min)
How to study each module: Read the lesson → say the rule out loud in your own words → tick “I can explain this” → answer the quiz without looking → revisit anything you miss. Aim for 90% on practice as a study goal, not as a claim about the state’s required passing score.

Fast memory anchors

Right-of-way

First to stop; if simultaneous, farthest right; if directly opposing and one turns left, straight traffic generally goes first. Yield if uncertain.

Signals

Red = stop. Yellow = stop if safe. Flashing red = stop sign. Flashing yellow = slow/caution. Green does not mean drive into a blocked intersection.

Distance

At least 2 seconds in ideal conditions; more in rain, darkness, poor visibility, towing, or behind motorcycles. Motorcycle gap: 4–6 seconds.

Numbers

Signal at least 100 feet before turning/lane changes; headlights 30 minutes after sunset and 30 minutes before sunrise, or when visibility is poor; school-bus opposite-direction exception requires a median at least 20 feet wide.

Construction and work zones

Traffic lights

Steady red

Stop. Do not proceed until permitted and safe.

Steady yellow

The signal is changing to red. Stop if you can do so safely; do not accelerate to “beat” it.

Steady green

Proceed only if intersection is clear; yield to traffic/pedestrians already lawfully in it.

Flashing red

Same as a stop sign: complete stop, then go only when safe.

Flashing yellow

Slow down and proceed cautiously.

Arrows

Red arrow: no movement in arrow direction. Yellow arrow: signal is changing; stop if safe. Flashing yellow arrow: turn allowed after yielding. Green arrow: protected turn in that direction.

Sunday-night / Monday-morning final review

Know without hesitation

  • Red / yellow / green and flashing signals
  • Sign shapes and color families
  • School bus stop rule and 20-foot median exception
  • Stop-sign right-of-way order
  • Solid vs. broken white/yellow markings
  • 100-foot signal rule and 2-second following rule
  • Headlights when wipers needed and around sunrise/sunset
  • Truck no-zones; motorcycle following gap
  • Phone restrictions, alcohol/drug impairment
  • Crash response and report thresholds in the guide

Test strategy

  • Read the whole question; notice words like “except,” “must,” and “when safe.”
  • For right-of-way, choose the option that prevents conflict and obeys the control.
  • For any hazard, safest answer usually involves slowing, increasing space, yielding, or stopping when required.
  • Do not choose speeding up, forcing a gap, passing over a solid line, driving around gates, or using a phone.
  • If uncertain, eliminate obviously unsafe answers, then choose the legal and cautious action.
  • Get sleep. A tired brain makes careless mistakes.
